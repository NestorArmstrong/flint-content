# Why The World’s Best Stock Picker Advises You Not To Pick Stocks 

Warren Buffett, often hailed as the world’s most famous stock picker, has a surprisingly paradoxical piece of advice for regular investors: avoid picking stocks on your own. Instead, he suggests investing in index funds. This recommendation might seem counterintuitive coming from someone who has built his fortune on savvy stock picks, but there are several reasons why the Oracle of Omaha thinks this holds true for most people.

Buffett’s main argument centers around the complexity and challenges of successful stock picking. Investing on individual stocks requires a deep understanding of companies, their industries, and the broader economic environment. Even with this knowledge, predicting market movements and the future performance of specific stocks is extremely difficult. Most individual investors lack the time, resources, and expertise to consistently make good stock picks.

We just have to see the performance of professional fund managers to illustrate his point. Despite their expertise and resources, many professional fund managers fail to consistently outperform the market. And if the ‘pros’ struggle, it’s even more daunting for amateur investors to succeed at stock picking. In a 2016 letter to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders, Buffett highlighted how hedge funds and high-fee investment managers often fail to beat low-cost index funds over the long term.

The data doesn’t lie. Studies have shown that the majority of actively managed mutual funds do not outperform their benchmark indexes after fees are taken into account. According to the SPIVA (S&P Indices Versus Active) scorecard, over the long term, a significant percentage of actively managed funds underperform their respective benchmarks. This suggests that even the professionals have a hard time beating the market, reinforcing the advice for regular investors to avoid stock picking.

Index funds typically have lower fees than actively managed funds because they passively track a market index, such as the S&P 500, rather than trying to pick winning stocks. Lower fees mean more of your money is actually invested and growing over time. Additionally, index funds provide instant diversification, spreading your investment across many different companies and sectors, which reduces risk compared to investing on individual stocks.

Warren has actually put money where his mouth is. In his 2013 letter to Berkshire Hathaway shareholders, he revealed that he has instructed the trustee for his wife’s inheritance to invest 90% of her money in a low-cost S&P 500 index fund and 10% in short-term government bonds. This simple strategy reflects his belief in the long-term performance of the stock market and the difficulty of beating it through individual stock picking.

Finally, there is an advantage by staying invested for the long term. Market fluctuations are normal, and trying to time the market or pick the next hot stock can lead to poor decisions driven by raw emotions rather than logic. Index funds encourage a buy-and-hold strategy, which aligns with Buffett’s own investment philosophy of finding good investments and sticking with them.

---

### Should you stock pick?

To summarize, it seems that regular investors should refrain from picking stocks on their own because it is difficult, time-consuming, and often less profitable than simply investing in low-cost index funds. By following Warren’s advice, investors can benefit from the long-term growth of the market, reduce risk through diversification, and avoid the high fees and potential pitfalls of active stock picking. This straightforward approach could help you achieve better financial outcomes with less stress and effort.

Contrary to a conventional job, trying to work harder at investing won’t always yield you better results. Instead, you should aim to work smarter in order to achieve a better future for both yourself and your family.
