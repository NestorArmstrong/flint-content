# It’s Not the Portfolio. It’s You.

When investment performance disappoints, the most basic instinct is to adjust the portfolio. And so, investors change their assets around hoping that this time they’ll get it right. 

Yes, there can be times that a portfolio has issues. But usually that’s not the problem. 

As we all know, markets move in cycles. Periods of growth are followed by corrections. Volatility is not an anomaly, it’s just a part of the process. And yet many investors treat it as a signal to *act*.

They sell when markets fall, trying to avoid further losses. They also buy when markets rise, because they’re afraid of missing out. The combination of these behaviors create a very destructive pattern: selling low and buying high.

Each new reaction introduces more friction. Every decision you make under stress tend to be extremely short sighted, even when the original strategy was long term. Over time, such damage compounds and erodes your returns.

*This is not just a theory.* Many studies have consistently shown that the average investor underperforms the very funds they invest in. The reason behind this is that consistent bad timing decimates any chance for proper investment growth. 

So instead of trying to find the perfect portfolio, focus on **building one you can actually keep for long periods of time**. You’ll also save yourself a lot of headaches by **learning that swings in price are merely part of the process**, and not permanent failure. Finally, make peace with the fact that **doing nothing is often the hardest and most profitable decision**.

Because what breaks most portfolios isn’t what’s in them, but how investors behave when it matters most.
